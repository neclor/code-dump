\subsection*{B.4 \quad The abstraction permit $L$}

The current basis stays optimal for
\[
\mathbf{9\,495.5\ \le L\le 11\,000}\qquad(\Delta L\in[-504.5,\,+1000]).
\]
Inside, the plan is a straight line in $L$. Each $+1\,000$ m$^3$ moves these areas; all others stay fixed:

\input{tables/b_rates}

The extra water irrigates maize on B3 ($500$ m$^3$/ha) instead of rain-fed barley. Maize needs $1.8$ h/ha more than barley in autumn, where labour is exhausted, so beet on B2 ($7.5$ h/ha in autumn) turns into barley ($3.2$ h/ha): $4.02\cdot 1.8=1.68\cdot 4.3=7.2$ h. Water closes too: $4.02\cdot 500-1.68\cdot 600=1\,000$ m$^3$. The margin grows by $0.60$ EUR per m$^3$, the price of the permit.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Above 11\,000:} nothing changes. The margin is flat at $122\,677.73$ EUR (slope 0).
\item \textbf{Below 9\,495.5:} the basis changes and the margin falls faster. The slope is 0.61, then 0.69 (down to $L\approx 6\,500$), then rises to 2.83 EUR/m$^3$ (below $L\approx 6\,000$).\end{itemize}

The margin is concave in $L$: water goes to its best use first. The price $0.60$ is valid only inside the interval.
