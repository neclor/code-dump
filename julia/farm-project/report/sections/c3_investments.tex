\subsection*{C.3 \quad Three investments}

\textbf{Method.} For each resource the program buys $1,2,3,\dots$ units, solves the three years again and records the gain of the discounted margin. Gain per unit and per year (the discounted gain divided by $1+1.03^{-1}+1.03^{-2}=2.9135$) is the \emph{value of a unit}. An investment pays while its price is below this value (Figure~\ref{fig:invest}).

\input{figures/c_invest}

The first units are the most valuable; then the value falls. Ranking by the first unit: land B1 (836 EUR/ha) $>$ B4 (794) $>$ B2 (690) $>$ B3 (597); autumn hours saved (24 EUR/h); reservoir (0.72 EUR/m$^3$); permit (0.21 EUR/m$^3$).

\textbf{Assumptions} (not in the data):
\begin{itemize}
\item land: rent 400 EUR/ha per year, 3-year contract; the parcel lies next to B1 and has the same soil (deep loam); the 5 ha were under cover fallow in year 0; the shares of the farm (diversification, fallow, legume) are computed from 85 ha;
\item reservoir: 6 EUR per m$^3$, life 20 years, paid as an annuity at 3\%: 604.94 EUR per year for 1\,500 m$^3$;
\item machine (for example a harvester) that saves 100 autumn hours per year: price 30\,000 EUR, life 10 years, paid as an annuity at 3\%: 3\,516.92 EUR per year.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Rent B1: yes.} 5 ha is the amount where the next hectare ($377$) is worth less than the rent ($400$).
\item \textbf{Bigger reservoir: yes}, up to 1\,500 m$^3$.
\item \textbf{Machine: no.} A harvester costs about 30\,000 EUR (10 years, 3\%), i.e.\ $10\,246.43$ EUR over the three years, more than the $6\,992.69$ EUR it saves. It would pay only below $20\,470$ EUR.
\end{itemize}
The decisions depend on the assumed prices; the curves show the answer for any other price.
